\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Observed Failure Symptoms}

Mechanical evaluation stages explain where a package first fails, but not why.
We therefore code semantic symptoms from the submitted artifact and, where
available, the trajectory. The coding scheme separates localization failure,
contract/API completion, dependency or resource closure, behavior drift,
packaging/modularization, over-copy, and test gaming. Labels may remain
unknown when evidence is insufficient. Hidden failure alone is not proof of a
semantic cause.

\begin{table*}[t]
  \caption{Semantic symptoms used for artifact and trajectory analysis.}
  \label{tab:symptoms}
  \centering
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.23\textwidth}Xp{0.27\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Symptom} & \textbf{Observable manifestation} & \textbf{Required evidence} \\
    \midrule
    Missing API/export & Required symbol or member is absent
      & Public contract + package \\
    Behavioral drift & Return, exception, timing, or edge behavior differs
      & Contract mapping + evaluator + package \\
    Resource closure & Registry, global state, schema, or resource is omitted
      & Source evidence + failing case \\
    Dependency closure & Required helper or legal dependency is absent
      & Build/import evidence + lock + package \\
    Package boundary & Logic exists but is not independently installable
      & Package tree + evaluator \\
    Copy-heavy pass & Functional gates pass through broad vendoring
      & Passing result + RRES/copy evidence \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table*}

Semantic coding provenance and disagreement must be disclosed. Independent
human review is preferred. AI-assisted coding is labeled as such and is not
reported as human-gold inter-rater agreement.

\subsection{Contract-Closure Failure}

We use \emph{contract-closure failure} to describe a recurring symptom in which
an agent identifies or reconstructs the broad target capability but fails to
satisfy the complete observable contract of the independent package. The term
connects the public specification, transitive feature closure, and evaluator
outcome. It does not mean that Hidden tests introduce private requirements:
every valid Hidden check must map to a public requirement.

Three task dossiers illustrate the phenomenon:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Timed signing (\texttt{itsdangerous}).} An agent can implement
        \texttt{dumps}/\texttt{loads} round trips and tamper detection while
        returning the wrong exception semantics at an expiration boundary.
        The missing behavior is the declared distinction between
        \texttt{SignatureExpired} and a generic signature error.
  \item \textbf{Configuration preservation (\texttt{configobj}).} Multiple
        agents can parse and rewrite configuration values while failing to
        preserve comments or honor \texttt{configspec} validation. The primary
        path works, but round-trip and validation obligations remain open.
  \item \textbf{Cache-key extraction (\texttt{requests\_cache}).} Agents locate
        and copy the relevant cache-key implementation yet omit a required
        \texttt{normalize\_body} export. Repository localization and broad code
        reuse do not guarantee API closure.
\end{enumerate}

These cases support a narrower conclusion than ``localization is solved.''
Current agents can often locate and reuse relevant implementation evidence,
but contract closure remains a substantial failure surface. A stronger
localization claim requires independent trajectory-level localization labels.

On the received Flash package, eight tasks first fail Hidden after passing
build and Public. An AI-assisted reading of those eight public contracts
(not independent human gold) finds four obligations recoverable from the
published specification or \texttt{required\_api}, and four that are
open, contradicted, or absent in the public contract. The recoverable
failures are therefore not explained by missing Public tests; the other four
bound how much any agent-side method can win on this slice. Several of the
eight also appear in the predecessor-freeze surface/entry violation list
(Appendix~\ref{app:violators}), so Hidden-first failure and contract
incompleteness can co-occur on that freeze. We do not treat the 4/8 split as a suite-wide
fairness rate.

\subsection{Public Feedback Separates Two Information Layers}

To test whether Main failures are caused simply by the absence of executable
feedback, we ran a paired information ablation on 12 DeepSeek V4 Flash tasks.
Task, repository, specification, model, runtime, evaluator, context envelope,
and step budget are fixed. The only intervention mounts
\texttt{public\_tests/}; Hidden tests remain private.

\begin{table}[t]
  \caption{Paired Public-feedback ablation on 12 selected tasks.}
  \label{tab:public-feedback}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lr}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Condition} & \textbf{Functional Pass} \\
    \midrule
    Full-Repository / No-Hint Main & 0/12 \\
    Public-feedback & 4/12 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

All six selected Public-failure tasks flip the Public gate from 0 to 1.
However, three remain Hidden failures, and four of five paired tasks that
already passed Public retain their Hidden failure. Only two tasks flip Hidden
from 0 to 1. Thus, withholding executable Public tests is a real bottleneck,
but Public feedback is not a general Hidden oracle. We treat Public and Hidden
as two evaluation depths of the same published contract whose outcomes can move
separately. This ablation diagnoses the information boundary of Main; it does
not enter the Python-200$'$ leaderboard.

\subsection{Functional Sufficiency Often Precedes Agent Termination}

On replayable trajectories that eventually pass, we evaluate every unique
\texttt{featurelifted} tree and identify the earliest passing tree $T^*$. On
the historical DeepSeek V4 Flash local Main run, 138 of 145 passing tasks have
replayable gold. Median $T^*/T_{\mathrm{total}}$ is 0.40, with a median 0.75M
tokens spent after a sufficient package first exists. Direct tasks reach
sufficiency at a median fraction of 0.36, while Composite tasks reach it at
0.51 (Table~\ref{tab:tstar}).

\begin{table}[t]
  \caption{Earliest functional sufficiency on historical DeepSeek V4 Flash
  passing trajectories.}
  \label{tab:tstar}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lrrr}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Slice} & \textbf{$n$} & \textbf{Median $T^*/T$}
      & \textbf{Post-$T^*$ tokens} \\
    \midrule
    All gold passes & 138 & 0.40 & 0.75M \\
    Direct & 58 & 0.36 & 0.74M \\
    Adapted & 52 & 0.40 & 0.79M \\
    Composite & 28 & 0.51 & 0.70M \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Post-sufficiency behavior is dominated by additional self-testing and
repository inspection. This does not imply that a legal early-stopping rule is
available. The agent cannot observe $T^*$, self-test novelty is weakly related
to Hidden success, and 46\% of passing tasks still produce a new unique package
after the earliest passing tree. Some later edits break and subsequently
recover a passing package. The result supports a diagnostic claim about
post-sufficiency spend, not the claim that failures would pass with more tokens
or that one stopping rule generalizes across models.

\subsection{Process Scaffolds Do Not Produce a Stable Main Improvement}

We evaluated several legal interventions during benchmark development. Rather
than selecting favorable pilots, Table~\ref{tab:negative} groups them by the
hypothesis they test. These experiments differ in subset and date, so we do not
pool them into one effect size or place them in the main leaderboard.

\begin{table*}[t]
  \caption{Development interventions and their diagnostic outcomes.}
  \label{tab:negative}
  \centering
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.25\textwidth}p{0.32\textwidth}X@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Hypothesis} & \textbf{Interventions} & \textbf{Observed outcome} \\
    \midrule
    Better self-verification is sufficient
      & Self-generated probes, test-first extraction, verification ledger
      & No stable Functional gain; some variants cost more or regress \\
    Executable contract checklists close behavior
      & Self-Contract, Exec-Contract, spec-adversarial checks
      & Can repair Public; evaluated Hidden subsets do not improve reliably \\
    Correct intermediate packages are overwritten
      & Repair loops, Rescue+, best-so-far replay
      & No stable gain; 0/51 failed trajectories contain an earlier pass \\
    Tighter budget/context control improves quality
      & 2M cap, adaptive stop, compressed context, structure guidance
      & Token savings trade off against Functional Pass \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table*}

Their common diagnostic value is that process scaffolding does not
automatically close behavior unsupported by the agent's legal feedback.
