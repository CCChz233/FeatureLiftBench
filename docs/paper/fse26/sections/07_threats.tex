\section{Threats to Validity and Responsible Release}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{Construct validity.}
Functional Pass is a deterministic but finite operationalization of behavioral
completeness. Public and Hidden tests are mapped to one published contract, but
finite tests cannot prove semantic equivalence. A Hidden requirement that is
not observable from the public contract is a task defect, not an agent failure.
The freeze~v2 200/0 labels measure whether Hidden uses undeclared members and whether
declared entry points resolve; they are not a semantic Hidden-fairness gold
set. A 20-task C1 sample of \texttt{meets\_standard} tasks found no additional
undeclared members, which is a detector precision/recall-sample note rather
than Hidden-fairness recall. Independent dual-agent provenance audit of the
Flash-33 Hidden-failure slice is incomplete (Gate~0 smoke produced a valid
record but not the required runner exit; Gate~1 covers 3+10 tasks, not 33/33)
and is not reported as gold. We therefore keep full, observable-only, and
conservative sensitivity views as planned analyses rather than current
results. Functional Pass is binary and does not give headline partial credit.
\rres, copied-code fraction, files, and dependency footprint are proxies that
may miss semantic copying or penalize legitimate alternative implementations.

\paragraph{Internal validity.}
Results depend on source materialization, locked dependencies, prompt and
context policy, agent/evaluator containers, model endpoints, and runner state.
The received 2026-08-29 candidate demonstrates how freeze mismatches, missing
offline wheels, and context-policy violations can contaminate a nominal score.
A later 84-task replacement directory is incomplete (38/84) and uses local
\texttt{latest} image digests that do not match the received-suite pins
(Appendix~\ref{app:artifact}); we do not merge it. We therefore separate
infrastructure from model outcomes and require source, dependency, context,
provenance, and image attestations for the paper bundle. Method pilots differ
in subset and date; they support mechanism hypotheses but are not pooled into
a causal estimate. The 81/96 slice is a sensitivity cut of already-eligible
rows, not a repaired 200-task run.

\paragraph{External validity.}
Python-200$'$ is a maintainer- and AI-assisted selection of Python libraries,
tooling, and framework components. It is not a random sample of software
repositories and does not establish performance on large product applications,
GUI software, GPU systems, distributed services, or non-Python ecosystems.
Popular upstream projects may also appear in model training data. Repository
diversity does not guarantee balanced behavior or entanglement mechanisms.

\paragraph{Reliability and statistical conclusion validity.}
The main table initially uses one attempt per task. Without repeated trials it
does not estimate stochastic run-to-run variance. We report binomial confidence
intervals for aggregate rates and exact task-level outcomes for paired
comparisons, but neither replaces repeated-run sensitivity. A stratified
24-task stability study is deferred. Model APIs and serving stacks may change
despite stable model names. We record exact dates, profiles, source freezes,
and container identities.

\paragraph{Annotation validity.}
Task taxonomy, closure records, semantic failure labels, clause-narrowing
codes, and Flash-33 provenance labels include AI-assisted curation. We
disclose provenance and unknown rates and do not report these labels as
independent human gold. Inter-rater agreement is reported only when raters are
genuinely independent humans. The v2 surface/entry adjudications are
maintainer decisions over mechanical hits, not Hidden-semantics gold.

\paragraph{Runtime dependence.}
Official Main fixes OpenHands. Scores may change under another runtime, tool
interface, context policy, or recovery strategy. DeepSeek Harness and Codex
adapters exist but have no paired Core-12 scores; runtime comparisons remain
separate from the model leaderboard unless evaluated with a controlled paired
protocol.

\paragraph{Licensing and release.}
Upstream repositories retain heterogeneous licenses. Release packaging, source
acquisition, evaluator-side references, and generated submissions must preserve
license obligations. Large source and result archives may be distributed via
checksummed acquisition instructions rather than committed directly.
